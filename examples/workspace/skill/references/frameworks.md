# Frameworks

> Named structures from The Decision Notebook, with inputs, steps, and
> outputs, plus provenance.

### F1. The Bet Anatomy
- type: SOURCE FACT
- source: chapter 1, section "Every Decision Is a Bet"
- source_confidence: high

A decomposition of any decision into three parts:
- **Inputs:** the decision situation.
- **Parts:** (1) the belief the bet rests on, (2) the stake, (3) the odds
  implicitly accepted.
- **Output:** an explicit bet statement ("we are betting __ because we
  believe __").
Use it whenever a decision is discussed only in terms of options - the
anatomy forces the hidden belief into the open where it can be examined.

### F2. The Decision Journal
- type: SOURCE FACT
- source: chapter 4, section "The Decision Journal"
- source_confidence: high

An external record written BEFORE outcomes are known.
- **Inputs:** a significant decision.
- **Five fields:** (1) the decision stated as a bet; (2) beliefs + confidence
  numbers; (3) rejected alternatives and why; (4) missing information you
  wish you had; (5) tripwires that would signal the bet failing.
- **Cadence:** one entry per significant decision; monthly review of three
  old entries scored on decision soundness, outcome informativeness, and
  confidence calibration.
- **Output:** accumulated calibration data and immunity to hindsight.

### F3. The Premortem
- type: SOURCE FACT
- source: chapter 6, section "Premortems and Tripwires"
- source_confidence: high

A pre-commitment failure rehearsal.
- **Inputs:** the decision, the team, 18 months of imagination.
- **Steps:** assume the decision has already failed badly; write the failure
  story in past tense with specifics; share aloud as a team; convert each
  named failure mode into a checklist item or a tripwire.
- **Output:** failure modes surfaced while they are still cheap to prevent,
  and a checklist nobody has to be brave to raise later.
- Grounded in the prospective-hindsight effect (P9).

### F4. The Tripwire
- type: SOURCE FACT
- source: chapter 6, section "Premortems and Tripwires"
- source_confidence: high

A pre-agreed signal that turns monitoring into a decision.
- **Three parts:** a measurable condition, a date, and an action
  ("if churn > 4% by end of Q2 → pause expansion and diagnose").
- **Why it works:** written by the still-objective pre-commitment self; it
  counters escalation of commitment and status-quo drift.
- **Output:** outcomes produce choices, not just meetings.

### F5. The Group Dissent Protocol
- type: SOURCE FACT
- source: chapter 7, section "Deciding in Groups: Permission to Disagree"
- source_confidence: high

For group decisions threatened by premature agreement or non-convergence.
- **Steps:** (1) collect estimates privately BEFORE discussion; reveal all
  at once - the spread is information the first speaker would destroy;
  (2) assign a rotating dissent slot: one person argues the strongest case
  against, in full, and the decision maker must respond in one sentence out
  loud; (3) close with a declared convergence state: decided /
  decided-pending-one-named-piece-of-information / deferred-with-date.
- **Output:** disagreement priced in; convergence explicit.

### F6. Sizing sentence pattern
- type: DISTILLER INFERENCE
- derived_from: P6, F1

A one-sentence sizing template the agent can hand to users:
"If I am wrong, this costs me X, and X is / is not survivable; my evidence
is thin / medium / strong, so my stake is small / medium / large."
It operationalizes P6 into a fill-in-the-blank check that runs in seconds.
