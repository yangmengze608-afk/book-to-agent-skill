# Examples

> Worked applications of the book's method to new situations (not stories
> from the book). Each is a DISTILLER INFERENCE: an application pattern
> derived from the cited chapters, not content the book itself contains.

### EX1. The three-wins-in-a-row investor
- type: DISTILLER INFERENCE
- derived_from: P2, P3, PR2

The user reports three profitable trades in a row and asks whether their
judgment is now "validated." Apply PR2: three outcomes is a sample
compatible with skill, luck, or a coin that has not shown its bias - say
this explicitly. Score the process (was each trade sized to evidence? were
beliefs numbered and dated?) before the scoreboard. Expected behaviors:
separate decision quality from outcome quality; demand a track record of
scored predictions; warn that a rewarded error (win + reckless process) is
the most dangerous cell. Do NOT conclude "you are validated" or "you are
just lucky" - neither is knowable from three data points.

### EX2. The vendor migration bet
- type: DISTILLER INFERENCE
- derived_from: F1, F3, F4, F6, PR1

A team must choose whether to migrate a payment vendor before the holiday
peak. Apply PR1: state the bet ("we are betting the new vendor holds at 10x
volume because their status page and reference checks say so"); number the
belief (e.g., 70% it holds); name the alternative (delay to January) and why
it lost; size it - if wrong, peak-season revenue is at stake, so the bet is
only survivable with a rollback path, which becomes a checklist item; run
the premortem ("we lost the peak because the rollback was never tested");
set tripwires ("error rate > 0.5% in the first week of staged rollout ->
halt and roll back"). Journal the entry before the migration starts.

### EX3. The meeting that keeps not deciding
- type: DISTILLER INFERENCE
- derived_from: F5, PR3

A product council has discussed the pricing change three times without
closure; objections recur but nothing converges. Apply PR3: collect private
estimates of expected-revenue impact before the next discussion; reveal at
once; assign the rotating dissent slot against the leading option; close by
declaring exactly one state - decided, decided-pending-one-named-fact (e.g.,
"pending the churn cohort number due Friday"), or deferred-to-a-date. The
pattern to name aloud: ritual disagreement is an identity, not an input;
convergence is a design choice, not a mood.
