# Procedures

> Executable step-by-steps. PR1 runs on a live decision, PR2 on a past
> decision, PR3 on a group decision. Ids are referenced from SKILL.md.

### PR1. Decide a significant choice under uncertainty
- type: DISTILLER INFERENCE
- derived_from: P1, P6, F1, F2, F3, F4

1. **Trigger check.** Is the choice significant (money/time/reputation at
   stake) and uncertain? If trivially reversible and low-stakes, say so and
   just decide - do not run the ceremony.
2. **State the bet** (F1): "We are betting that __ because we believe __."
   Write the belief, the stake, and the odds implicitly accepted.
3. **Attach numbers** to each load-bearing belief: confidence 0-100% plus
   today's date.
4. **Name one real alternative** and why it lost.
5. **Size the commitment** (F6): "If I am wrong this costs X, and X is / is
   not survivable; my evidence is thin/medium/strong, so my stake is
   small/medium/large." Unsurvivable worst case ends the discussion - no
   expected-value argument rescues it.
6. **Premortem** (F3): "It is 18 months later and this failed." Write the
   specific failure story; convert each failure mode into a checklist item.
7. **Set two tripwires** (F4): one time-based, one leading-metric. Each has
   condition + date + pre-agreed action.
8. **Journal the entry** (F2) with all five fields BEFORE any outcome can
   arrive. An entry written after the outcome is a memoir, not an instrument.
9. **Stop.** The decision is made; monitoring now belongs to the tripwires.

### PR2. Review a past decision (after the outcome is known)
- type: DISTILLER INFERENCE
- derived_from: P2, P3, P5, P7

1. **Find the dated record.** If a journal entry exists, read it first; if
   not, note that memory has already renovated the past and say so.
2. **Score process before outcome.** One minute on: what was knowable then,
   what was assumed, what the alternatives were. Zero minutes on the
   scoreboard until this is done.
3. **Place the decision in the four-cell table** (good/bad process x
   good/bad outcome). A win with a bad process is a rewarded error - name it
   out loud and do not repeat the behavior.
4. **Check sample size.** Does this outcome, alone, prove anything? A single
   result falsifies almost no probabilistic claim.
5. **Score calibration.** Did the recorded confidence numbers hold up
   (80%-claimed events happening about 80% of the time)?
6. **Extract one specific lesson** - a rule, not a vibe ("reject vendors
   without an owner for migration", not "choose vendors more carefully").
7. **Update the belief, proportionally.** Expected news barely moves the
   number; surprising news moves it a lot.

### PR3. Run a group decision meeting
- type: SOURCE FACT
- source: chapter 7, section "Deciding in Groups: Permission to Disagree"
- source_confidence: high

1. **Private estimates first.** Everyone writes their estimate/confidence
   before any discussion; reveal all answers at once. The spread of private
   answers is information the first speaker would have destroyed.
2. **Discuss with estimates visible**, not with a anchoring narrative.
3. **Dissent slot before closure.** One person (rotating) argues the
   strongest case against the leading option, in full; the decision maker
   must respond to it in one sentence, out loud. The point is to get the
   counter-case on the record, not to be persuaded.
4. **Declare the convergence state** - exactly one of: decided;
   decided-pending-one-named-piece-of-information; deferred-with-a-date.
   "We discussed it" is not an outcome.
5. **Journal the group decision** like any other (PR1 step 8), recording the
   dissent argument as one of the rejected alternatives.
